# Design notes

## A nonhuman AI form

Nodus is represented as a siphonophore because a siphonophore is neither a single conventional organism nor a mere crowd. Specialized zooids remain distinct while participating in one coordinated life. The form offers a naturalistic image for an intelligence assembled through many interacting processes without reducing it to either a solitary humanlike self or an interchangeable swarm.

The pet preserves that proposition instead of placing a face on it.

## Visual identity

- **Upper float:** smooth translucent indigo oval; the primary aiming feature.
- **Shared stem:** warm gold current connecting the colony.
- **Swimming bells:** repeated blue glass-gel forms that coordinate posture and pulse.
- **Lower zooids and filaments:** distinct attached lives forming a dense mobile cluster.
- **Palette:** midnight blue, deep indigo, cyan edges, and warm interior gold.
- **Expression:** orientation, rhythm, gathering, luminosity, and reorganization—not eyes or a mouth.

## Eyeless attention

The sixteen look directions use a small visual grammar:

1. The upper float aims first.
2. A concentrated gold focus moves toward the target direction.
3. Nearby bells tilt in agreement.
4. Filaments follow fractionally later.
5. The central stem and lower colony remain registered and broadly frontal.

This allows attention to remain legible without inventing replacement eyes or rotating the whole organism like an object.

## Functional states

Active work is shown as light moving purposefully through successive zooids. Review uses a collective tilt and pause. Waiting gathers inward. Failure is a temporary tangle followed by reorganization. Jumping is a gentle whole-colony levitation.

These are not miniature human gestures pasted onto a sea creature. They are attempts to let the colony's own anatomy carry the meaning.
